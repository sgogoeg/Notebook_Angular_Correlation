\subsection{Gain of the amplifiers}
Using the circuit from Fig.~\ref{fig:circ_01}, we first connected the output of the amplifier to the oscilloscope and adjusted the gain such that amplitude of the bulk of the cleanly shaped signals would have an amplitude between \qtyrange{8}{9}{\volt}, in accordance with the 10 \unit{\volt} maximum of the 
multi-channel analyzer (MCA). On the oscilloscope, we could see a bipolar pulse per signal, as shown in Fig. \ref{fig:osc_amp}. Some signals of higher amplitude (surpassing the 9 \unit{\volt}) where also visible, however their shape differed compared to that of Fig. \ref{fig:osc_amp}. This suggested the events correspond to simultaneous absorptions of multiple photons, or a result of pile-up effects, making their non-detection at the MCA desirable. 

The amplitude seen in the oscilloscope is proportional to the energy deposited in the detectors, and no saturation (manifesting as a plateau in the analog signal) was observed. 
By switching the gain output from the oscilloscope to the MCA with the gate off, we could also check that the full spectrum was visible in the software by comparing with the spectrum from the literature \cite{Amato:2022yoj}

\subsection{Calibration of the SCAs}
The single-channel-analyzer (SCA) needs to be calibrated, once the analysis will not be done in the whole spectrum. 
In order to achieve that, the circuit from Fig. \ref{fig:circ_02} is used.
The signal coming from the amplifier is divided, one goes to the delay generator and the other goes to the SCA followed by a gate-delay-generator (GDG). 
They are then connected to either the oscilloscope or to the MCA.
The delay and the GDG are selected such that the analog signal is aligned with the digital, without cutting the peaks, according to Fig. \ref{fig:osc_SCA}. This is done using the oscilloscope.

\subsection{Delay in the fast branch}
The fast branch of each detector has its own CFD and delay, as in the circuit \ref{fig:circ_04}.
The signals must arrive at the same at the fast coincidence (FC) and then we can build the prompt curve, Fig. \ref{fig:prompt_curve}, by changing the delay times of each detector \cite{Siegbahn1965}.
First, the delay on detector 01 (the movable one) was kept to 0 and we changed the delay the on the detector 02 (fixed detector), which is the positive part of the prompt curve.
After this, the opposite was done. 
The delays from detector 02 were set to zero and the delays on detector 01 were changed, resulting in the negative part of the prompt curve. 

The prompt curve is fitted with Eq. \ref{eq:fit_prompt}.
The resolving time changes the width of the prompt curve, the shorter it is, narrower the prompt curve becomes, as it controls the width of the curve.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fit_prompt}
    f(t) = \frac{A}{2}\cdot \left ( 1 + \mathrm{erf} \left(\dfrac{t-(t_{0}-\frac{w}{2})}{\sigma} \right) \cdot \mathrm{erf} \left(\dfrac{(t_{0}+\frac{w}{2})-t}{\sigma} \right)\right) + A_{0}
\end{equation}
were $A$ is the height of the prompt curve, $t_0$ is the center in time, $w = 2 \tau$ is the resolving time of the setup, $\sigma$ controls the steepness of the rise and fall of the curve and $A_0$ is the random coincidence background of the curve.

\subsection{Delay in the slow branch}
Lastly, the delay in the slow branch needs to be set. 
The circuit from Fig. \ref{fig:circ_final} is used and it is also the circuit used for the main measurement. 
Each detector has a slow-branch with an amplifier, SCA, delay and the output goes to a counter and to the universal coincidence (UC). 
Also, each detector has a fast-branch with the inverter amplifier, CFD and delays. 
The output of both fast-branches go to the fast-coincidence (FC), which then goes to a counter and to the GDG that connects to the UC. 
In the end, the UC has three inputs: two from the slow-branches and one from fast-branch (CFDs, FC and GDG).

To define the correct delay, we used the oscilloscope.
The channel 1 of the oscilloscope is connected to the slow-branch of detector 01, the channel 3 is connected to the slow-branch of detector 02 and the channel 4 is connected to the GDG that is connected to the fast coincidence. 
The channel 2 of the oscilloscope is not used because the color makes it harder to visualize the signal. 
The signals were set to be center aligned, as seen in Fig. \ref{fig:slow_delay} \footnote{We had to use the picture because the oscilloscope was not saving the screenshot anymore. We went to the laboratory again when we noticed the issue, but it persisted and we opted for the picture.}, to maximize the coincidence, as it assures the slow-branches are within the gate.

\subsection{Measurement of the angular correlation}

The angular correlation function $W(\theta)$ is defined as:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:angular_correlation}
    W(\theta) = K_0\,[1 + A_{22}^{\text{exp}} P_2(\cos\theta) + A_{44}^{\text{exp}} P_4(\cos\theta)] \, ,
\end{equation}

where $P_n$ is the n-th Legendre polynomials. They codify the angular dependence in the reaction. $K_0$ is a proportionality constant that absorbs parameters like the level of activity of the source.

Theoretical coefficients for the cascade are related to experimentally observed ones by a solid angle attenuation coefficient $Q_{kk}$ by $A_{kk} = A_{kk}^\mathrm{exp} / Q_{kk}$ \cite{Siegbahn1965}. Both detectors were measured to be at $5.1 \pm  0.1 \, \unit{cm}$, indicating the appropriate $Q_{kk}$.

Fits for eq~\ref{eq:angular_correlation}, alongside the theoretical angular distribution for the solid angle corrected $W(\theta)$ for 4-2-0 and 0-1-0 cascades are shown in figure~\ref{fig:Count_rates}. The fit parameters, alongside $Q_{kk}$ and the solid angle corrected theoretical coefficients can be seen in table~\ref{tab:fit_angular_correlation}. The obtained values fall outside the theoretical expectations, even when considering the statistical uncertainties after multiple days of measurements. Previous measurements \cite{10.1093/ptep/ptag076, PhysRev.91.616}, with and without scintillator detectors confirm the theoretical values.
