\subsection{Gain of the amplifiers}
\textcolor{red}{Explain how you concluded that the
shown signals correspond to the photopeaks, how the signal shape should be, and how it would look like if saturated}

Using the circuit from Fig. \ref{fig:circ_01}, we first connected the output of the amplifier to the oscilloscope and adjusted the gain such that amplitude of the signal would be between \qtyrange{8}{9}{\volt}, because the multi-channel analyzer (MCA) has a maximum limit of 10 \unit{\volt}. 
On the oscilloscope, we could see a bipolar pulse per signal, as shown in Fig. \ref{fig:osc_amp}. 
The amplitude seen in the oscilloscope is proportional to the energy deposited in the detectors and there was no saturation because there was no plateau in the analog signal. 
By switching the gain output from the oscilloscope to the MCA with the gate off, we could also check that the full spectrum was visible in the software by comparing with the spectrum from the literature.

\subsection{Calibration of the SCAs}
\textcolor{red}{Indicate which thresholds were chosen for the final measurement. The sensitivity of the SCA should be close to 100 \% in the range of the photo peaks. Is it?}

The single-channel-analyzer (SCA) needs to be calibrated, once the analysis will not be done in the whole spectrum. 
In order to achieve that, the circuit from Fig. \ref{fig:circ_02} is used.
The signal coming from the amplifier is divided, one goes to the delay generator and the other goes to the SCA followed by a gate-delay-generator (GDG). 
They are then connected to either the oscilloscope or to the MCA.
The delay and the GDG are selected such that the analog signal is aligned with the digital, without cutting the peaks, according to Fig. \ref{fig:osc_SCA}
This is done using the oscilloscope.

On the other hand, while using the MCA, the SCA window is selected to contain both photopeaks of the spectrum for higher statistics.
For the fixed detector the SCA window was of lower-limit $9.20$ \unit{\volt} and $\Delta E = 2.30$ \unit{\volt}. 
For the movable detector, it was set to $8.40$ \unit{\volt} and $\Delta E = 2.52$ \unit{\volt}. 
The spectrum was recorded without the SCA gate (MCA self-gated, showing the full spectrum) and with the SCA gate, showing the peak selection, as shown in Fig. \ref{fig:cali_SCA}

As seen, for the fixed detector (detector 1) the sensitivity is above $1.0$ around the peaks. 
We suspect it is caused by changing the gain while the data was being taken or the gated and self-gated spectrum were recorded for different configurations, which caused the peaks of both to not be aligned, agreeing with what is observed in Fig. \ref{subfig:sca_spectra01} around the peaks.

\subsection{Delay in the fast branch}
The fast branch of each detector has its own CFD and delay, as in the circuit \ref{fig:circ_04}.
The signals must arrive at the same at the fast coincidence (FC) and then we can build the prompt curve, Fig. \ref{fig:prompt_curve}, by changing the delay times of each detector \cite{Siegbahn1965}.
First, the delay on detector 01 (the fixed one) was kept to 0 and we changed the delay the on the detector 02 (movable detector), which is the positive part of the prompt curve.
After this, the opposite was done. 
The delays from detector 02 were set to zero and the delays on detector 01 were changed, resulting in the negative part of the prompt curve. 

The prompt curve is fitted with Eq. \ref{eq:fit_prompt}.
The resolving time changes the width of the prompt curve, the shorter it is, narrower the prompt curve becomes. 




\begin{equation}\label{eq:fit_prompt}
    f(t) = \frac{A}{2}\cdot \left ( 1 + \mathrm{erf} \left(\dfrac{t-(t_{0}-\frac{w}{2})}{\sigma} \right) \cdot \mathrm{erf} \left(\dfrac{(t_{0}+\frac{w}{2})-t}{\sigma} \right)\right) + A_{0}
\end{equation}

\end{equation}
